Two of your requirements are in conflict, and Microsoft says so

You asked which upload method I would use and why. The honest answer starts with why it is not the obvious one.

"Request files is available only if all of the following statements are true... Your admin has enabled Anyone links for SharePoint. Your admin has configured the View, edit, and upload permission for Anyone links."

and: turning off Anyone links "also disables Request files at the site, SharePoint, and OneDrive levels."

Microsoft, Enable file requests in SharePoint or OneDrive
Anyone links are anonymous. No sign-in, no identity, no audit trail beyond "someone used this link", and they work for whoever the URL gets forwarded to. Switching Request files on means setting tenant-wide external sharing to Anyone, the loosest of the four levels.

Your brief asks for "security settings for external sharing, guest access, link expiration, and MFA" on a site receiving financial statements, payroll reports and contracts. With Anyone links on, there is no sign-in for MFA to apply to. Any proposal offering you file request links and MFA on uploads has not reconciled those two.

What I would build instead

External sharing set to "New and existing guests", the second of the four levels. The client clicks your link, types their email, gets a six-digit code, and is in. The part most people miss: they do not need a Microsoft account. Entra B2B authenticates them with an emailed code when they do not already have one.

The honest cost is one extra screen, once. First upload: type an email address and a code. After that the browser remembers and it is drag-and-drop. For a practice receiving payroll files from other firms' owners, one code buys an attributable audit trail for every file that arrives. If a link to a client's financials ever reaches the wrong inbox, "the link was anonymous by design" is not a sentence you want to say to them.

Something changing right now that affects this

Microsoft is retiring SharePoint's own one-time passcode and moving external sharing to Entra B2B guest accounts, announced in Message Center MC1243549.

I am not going to quote you a cutover date. The published write-ups of that message disagree with each other, and Microsoft's preview article and the message-center summaries do not line up. Check the Message Center entry in your own tenant for the date that applies to you.

What matters for the build is the direction, not the date: anything built on the old passcode path is deprecated, and the design above is on Entra B2B, which is where Microsoft is going. If you had hired someone a year ago, this is the thing that would be breaking on you about now.

If you want the file request link anyway

It is your practice and your risk, and there is a defensible middle version: turn Anyone links on but scope them to one site, set a short expiry, restrict them to upload-only so nobody can read what is already there, and leave every other site at "New and existing guests". That is materially safer than switching Anyone on tenant-wide. I would still not recommend it, for the audit-trail reason, but say so and I will build it that way and document exactly what the exposure is.

What the approach does not depend on

  • No third-party app or add-on. SharePoint, Entra and Power Automate only
  • No per-client licence. Entra B2B guests do not consume a Microsoft 365 licence for this, so your client count can grow without your bill moving
  • No SharePoint Premium or Syntex, and no E5 anything

Delete client records 12 months after an engagement ends

Read literally, that is event-based retention, where a label's clock is started by a recorded event. I would not build it that way, for two reasons.

  • Licensing. Event-driven retention is a records-management capability and the guidance points at the E5 compliance tier rather than either Business plan. The sources for that are third-party licensing write-ups rather than Microsoft's own table, so I will call it strongly indicated rather than confirmed. Either way, you do not need it
  • It does not match how advisory engagements end. There is no row in a database that flips when an engagement is over. It is over when you decide it is
  Retention label:   "Client records - 12 months"
    Retain:          12 months
    Start the clock: WHEN LABELLED
    At the end:      delete automatically

  Applied by:  the "close engagement" step you already perform
Your deliverables already include a walkthrough showing how to close engagements. Applying the label becomes part of that step, so the clock starts the day you close. That is exactly what your requirement asks for, with no event registration and no E5. It is also more honest: tying the clock to the human act of closing means the two can never drift apart.

The closeout, as three steps

  1. set the client's checklist items to Complete
  2. apply the "Client records - 12 months" label
  3. remove the client's guest access from the site

Step 3 matters on its own. An engagement that ended should not leave a guest able to sign in for another year. The audit check flags guests on closed sites, so a missed step 3 surfaces rather than lingering.

This is the one setting in the build that destroys data on purpose, so it gets treated that way. Before it goes near a client we test it on a throwaway site with a short period, watch the file actually disappear, and confirm everything outside the labelled library is untouched. And the closeout procedure has a step zero: move anything you need to keep out of the client library first. The documentation says that twice.

Two things only you can decide

  • Does the 12 months run from closeout, or from the last upload? My design says closeout because that is what your brief describes
  • Is there a category you must keep longer? Engagement letters or conflict-check records sometimes outlive the working papers. I am not in a position to advise on your professional obligations, and I would rather ask than assume a 12-month delete covers everything
